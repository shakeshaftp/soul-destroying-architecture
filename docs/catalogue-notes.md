# Catalogue notes

Working notes on the starter comparison set in `data/source.csv`. Not published
— `docs/` is excluded from the site build.

## Facts to check before this is shared

The set was drafted quickly to give the site something real to render. The
well-known claims are solid; these are the ones I would not put in front of a
lawmaker unchecked. Each is in a caption or pairing note, so a correction is a
one-cell edit.

| Row | Claim | Why check it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `worship` / contrast | Liverpool Metropolitan Cathedral "the subject of later legal action over the defects" | The leaks and the mosaic and aluminium failures are well documented. The archdiocese's action against Gibberd is widely reported but I have not verified the outcome or the date. Soften or cite. |
| `housing-district` / contrast | Bijlmermeer, architect **Siegfried Nassuth** | Nassuth led the plan for Amsterdam's Public Works department, but the district had many hands. Consider "Siegfried Nassuth, Amsterdam Public Works" or leave blank. |
| `housing-district` / contrast | "some thirteen thousand homes" | The planned figure and the built figure differ. Pick one and say which. |
| `japan-station` / exemplar | Kanazawa Station, architect **left blank** | The Tsuzumi-mon gate and Motenashi Dome (2005) have named designers; I was not confident enough to assert one. Worth filling in. |
| `worship` / exemplar | Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, architect **left blank** | Several practices contributed over a long build. Fill in or describe rather than name. |
| `social-housing` / contrast | Robin Hood Gardens, "more than two hundred families" | Deliberately vague because I was unsure of the exact flat count. Replace with the real number. |
| `japan-station` / contrast | Kyoto Station, "some four hundred and seventy metres" | Commonly cited as 470m. Confirm against a primary source before it goes in a briefing. |

## Why these six pairs

The set is built to survive the standard rebuttal — that this is nostalgia
dressed up as policy.

- **penn-station** — the cleanest control available. Same city, same site, same
  railway, same purpose, thirty-eight years apart. Nothing varies but design.
- **town-hall** — deliberately argues against our own side. Aalto's Säynätsalo
  is as modernist as Boston City Hall and people are fond of it. If the set
  did not contain a pair like this, the project would be a style preference
  rather than an argument.
- **social-housing** — both were avant-garde municipal schemes by celebrated
  architects. One is loved by its residents and protected; the other was
  demolished while critics campaigned to save it. That asymmetry is the point.
- **worship** — a living tradition producing work at the highest level *now*,
  in 2007, against a modern cathedral whose fabric failed early. Answers the
  "tradition is over" and "beauty is unaffordable" objections at once.
- **japan-station** — two big modern stations dropped into historic Japanese
  cities within a decade of each other. Takes the argument entirely outside
  the Western canon.
- **housing-district** — the weakest control in the set, roughly thirty years
  between them, and the pairing note says so in public. Including one openly
  imperfect pair is cheap insurance against the accusation that the others
  were rigged.

## Gaps worth filling

- No Islamic example other than Abu Dhabi, and no Japanese *traditional*
  example — Katsura would need pairing carefully, since a seventeenth-century
  villa against anything modern breaks the fairness rule the set is built on.
- Nothing from Latin America or Africa.
- Nothing at the scale of an ordinary street, which is where most people
  actually experience architecture. The current set is all landmarks. A
  terraced street against a cul-de-sac of volume-built houses would do more
  work for the housing argument than any single building here.
